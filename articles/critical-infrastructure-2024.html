Critical Infrastructure Under Siege:
The 2024 Cyber Threat Intelligence Landscape

An intelligence-led analysis of the cyber threat landscape targeting critical national infrastructure — covering geopolitical drivers, threat actor profiles, notable incident patterns, key vulnerabilities, and a seven-pillar advisory framework for defending high-value environments.

2024 Research 12 min read Joe Smith-Essang Threat Intelligence

Scope & Methodology

This analysis synthesises intelligence from consulting engagement observations, open-source intelligence (OSINT), sector CERT advisories, and commercial threat intelligence sources. All client-identifying information has been anonymized. The recommendations are universally applicable regardless of geography or sector.

Executive Summary

The 2024 threat landscape for critical national infrastructure was defined by the intersection of geopolitical instability, ransomware-as-a-service industrialisation, and a surge in supply chain exploitation. Nation-state actors with documented ties to state intelligence apparatus expanded their operational footprint beyond traditional espionage targets into financial services, energy, and telecommunications networks. Concurrently, ransomware syndicates — operating with the commercial efficiency of technology businesses — drove double-extortion campaigns that generated significant disruption and regulatory exposure for their victims.

Three threat categories dominated: advanced persistent threats (APTs) pursuing strategic intelligence collection; ransomware operators monetizing access acquired by initial access brokers; and insider threats facilitated by inadequate access governance and weak separation of duties in privileged systems.

Geopolitical Context: When Conflicts Become Cyber Campaigns

The cyber domain in 2024 continued its role as the preferred asymmetric battlefield for state and non-state actors. Three geopolitical flashpoints directly shaped the regional threat landscape:

RUSSIA-UKRAINE CONFLICT

Russian APT groups expanded targeting beyond the conflict zone to allied and partner nations, including African financial and government institutions offering support to Ukraine. Destructive wiper malware and critical infrastructure sabotage TTPs observed in reconnaissance activities.

ISRAEL-HAMAS CONFLICT

Pro-Palestinian hacktivist groups and Iranian state-nexus actors intensified distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) campaigns and intrusion operations targeting financial services, media, and telecommunications providers globally.

REGIONAL SECURITY INSTABILITY

Political transitions and governance vacuums created conditions exploited by cyber mercenaries offering services to non-state clients. Critical infrastructure in energy and mining sectors became targets for economic disruption campaigns.

Notable Incident Patterns (Obfuscated Case Studies)

The following case studies are drawn from direct engagement observation and intelligence sources. All client-identifying information has been removed. The patterns, TTPs, and lessons are universally applicable.

Insider Financial Fraud — $29M Loss at a Major Financial Institution

SECTOR: Banking & Finance  |  THREAT TYPE: Insider Threat  |  IMPACT: $29M+

A privileged employee at a major financial institution exploited elevated access rights within the core banking system to manipulate fund settlement procedures over an extended period. The fraud was facilitated by inadequate separation of duties controls, lack of real-time transaction monitoring for insider behaviour, and a failure to enforce the principle of least privilege. The insider collaborated with an external criminal syndicate to route funds to accounts beyond the organization's jurisdiction.

Root cause: Absence of User and Entity Behaviour Analytics (UEBA); an employee role that combined initiation and approval privileges in the same account; no periodic access recertification process.

Patient Data Exfiltration — Healthcare Technology Provider

SECTOR: Health Services  |  THREAT TYPE: External Breach  |  IMPACT: PII/PHI Exposure

A regional health-technology startup operating electronic health record (EHR) services suffered a breach resulting in the exposure of patient personally identifiable information (PII) and protected health information (PHI). The data appeared on dark web forums within 72 hours of the breach, indicating the attacker had pre-arranged a buyer. The attack vector was an unpatched API endpoint with insufficient authentication controls.

Root cause: Inadequate API security posture; no penetration testing prior to production deployment; absence of dark web monitoring that would have provided earlier notification; non-compliance with applicable data protection legislation.

Double-Extortion Ransomware — State Government Administration

SECTOR: Government Services  |  THREAT TYPE: RaaS (FunkSec)  |  IMPACT: Service Disruption

A state government's administrative systems were encrypted by the FunkSec ransomware group in a double-extortion attack. The group exfiltrated citizen records and government operational data before deploying the encryption payload, publishing excerpts on their dark web leak site as proof of compromise. Government services were disrupted for several days while recovery operations were conducted.

Root cause: Outdated endpoint protection on legacy government workstations; flat network architecture enabling rapid lateral movement; absence of ransomware-specific IR playbook; online-only backup strategy (encrypted alongside production systems).

FunkSec: AI-Assisted Malware Development

FunkSec emerged in late 2024 as a novel RaaS group notable for reportedly leveraging generative AI to accelerate malware development. Analysis of their toolchain revealed code patterns inconsistent with manual authorship, suggesting AI-generated components. This trend — adversaries using LLMs to lower the technical barrier for malware creation — is expected to intensify.

Pattern Recognition

Every major CVE in 2024 targeted network edge infrastructure — VPNs, remote access tools, and firewall management interfaces. These are exactly the systems that cannot be protected by endpoint detection alone. Vulnerability management for edge devices must be treated as a board-level priority, not a routine patching task.

Risk Profile & Impact Scoring Methodology

A quantitative risk profile was developed to rank sector criticality and prioritise defensive resource allocation. The model combines threat exposure, asset criticality, and control effectiveness:

RISK PROFILE FORMULA

Risk Profile = ( T × V ) ÷ C

T = Threat Level (actor capability × intent)  |  V = Vulnerability Score  |  C = Control Effectiveness

BUSINESS IMPACT SCORE (BIS) FORMULA

BIS = ( BD×0.30 ) + ( DS×0.25 ) + ( FL×0.20 ) + ( RC×0.15 ) + ( RD×0.10 )

BD = Business Disruption  |  DS = Data Sensitivity  |  FL = Financial Loss
RC = Regulatory & Compliance exposure  |  RD = Reputational Damage

Business Disruption carries the highest weighting (30%) because downtime in critical sectors has cascading economic effects beyond the immediate victim. Data Sensitivity (25%) reflects the regulatory and reputational multiplier effect of personal data exposure.

Regulatory Landscape

The regulatory environment governing critical infrastructure security is intensifying globally. Organizations operating in or adjacent to CNII sectors face an expanding compliance burden:

  • Data Protection Legislation: National data protection acts now mandate breach notification within 72 hours in many jurisdictions. Non-compliance carries fines of up to 2% of annual turnover.
  • Cybercrime Amendments: Updated cybercrime legislation introduced offences specifically targeting attacks on CNII with substantially higher penalties and mandatory reporting obligations.
  • Sectoral Minimum Standards: Financial services regulators have issued technology risk management frameworks mandating SOC implementation, EDR deployment, and incident response capability for licensed institutions.
  • OT/ICS Security Guidelines: Energy and utility regulators are moving toward mandatory security assessments of operational technology (OT) environments following documented intrusions at power generation facilities.

Seven Advisories for Critical Infrastructure Defence

These advisories represent the distilled recommendations from this analysis, calibrated specifically for organizations operating within or serving critical national infrastructure:

01

Establish Proactive Threat Hunting & Intelligence Sharing

Do not wait for alerts — actively hunt for threat actor TTPs in your environment. Join sectoral ISACs, subscribe to government CERT advisories, and participate in intelligence-sharing consortiums. Threat intelligence that reaches your team before the attack changes the outcome fundamentally.

02

Harden Your Security Posture with Priority Focus on Edge Devices

Patch VPNs, firewalls, and remote access tools within 24-48 hours of critical CVE disclosure. Consider isolating management interfaces from internet exposure. Deploy cloud security posture management (CSPM) for cloud infrastructure. Enforce MFA universally. The 2024 CVE pattern clearly shows edge devices are the primary attack surface.

03

Address Insider Threats with Access Governance Controls

Implement user and entity behaviour analytics (UEBA), enforce separation of duties in high-value processes (especially financial settlement and privileged IT operations), conduct quarterly access recertification, and deploy PAM for privileged accounts. The $29M insider fraud case was entirely preventable with standard governance controls.

04

Build Ransomware Resilience as a Core Capability

Implement offline, immutable backups (air-gapped or WORM storage). Deploy EDR on all endpoints. Segment your network so that a single compromised workstation cannot reach your backup infrastructure. Conduct ransomware tabletop exercises at least annually. Brief your board on the decision framework for ransom payment vs. recovery — before an incident, not during one.

05

Strengthen Operational Technology (OT) Security

OT environments (SCADA, ICS, building management systems) are increasingly targeted and rarely hardened to IT security standards. Conduct asset inventory, segment OT from IT networks, and implement monitoring capable of detecting anomalous OT protocol behaviour. Nation-state actors specifically target OT for its physical disruption potential.

06

Accelerate Regulatory Compliance as a Security Investment

Compliance deadlines are a forcing function for security maturity. Use regulatory requirements to build the business case for security investments that would otherwise struggle for budget. Align your security roadmap to applicable standards (ISO 27001, NIST CSF, sectoral frameworks) and treat audits as maturity assessments, not bureaucratic obligations.

07

Combat Social Engineering with Culture, Not Just Technology

Phishing remains the primary initial access vector for both APTs and ransomware groups. Technology controls (email filtering, anti-phishing, MFA) reduce but do not eliminate the risk. Invest in security awareness training, simulate phishing campaigns to measure and improve employee susceptibility rates, and reward employees for reporting suspicious activity rather than penalising those who fall for tests.

Immediate Action Recommendations

For security teams and executives who need to act on this intelligence now:

Audit Edge Device Exposure

Run an immediate inventory of internet-facing VPNs, firewalls, and remote access tools. Check each against the 2024 CVE list above and patch or mitigate within 48 hours.

Review Privileged Access

Pull a report of all accounts with Domain Admin, Database Admin, or financial transaction approval rights. Remove unnecessary elevation. Enroll all privileged accounts in MFA and PAM vaulting.

Test Your Backup Restoration

Schedule a backup restoration test this quarter. Confirm that at least one backup copy is offline or air-gapped and validate that restoration time meets your Recovery Time Objective (RTO).

Subscribe to CERT Intelligence

Ensure your security team is subscribed to your national CERT advisory mailing list and at least one commercial threat intelligence feed relevant to your sector. Set up alerts for threat actors profiled above.

Conduct a Ransomware Tabletop

Run a one-day tabletop exercise simulating a ransomware scenario. Test your IR playbook, communication plan, and decision-making framework. Identify gaps before an attacker does.

Enable Dark Web Monitoring

Given the documented breach-to-dark-web timeline (as little as 72 hours), deploy dark web monitoring for corporate email domains, IP ranges, and sensitive asset names. See the companion article on dark web intelligence for a monitoring lifecycle framework.

Key Takeaways

  • Geopolitics is now your threat model — conflicts thousands of miles away generate real cyber threats to domestic infrastructure. Threat modelling must incorporate geopolitical context.
  • Edge devices are the new perimeter battleground — every major 2024 zero-day targeted VPNs, firewalls, and remote access infrastructure. Treat these as your highest-priority patch targets.
  • Insider threats are systemic, not isolated — access governance failures are the enabler. UEBA, PAM, and separation of duties are essential controls, not optional enhancements.
  • Ransomware is a business — RaaS groups operate with commercial precision. Match their operational efficiency with equally systematic defences: offline backups, EDR, network segmentation, and tested playbooks.
  • AI is coming to both sides — FunkSec's AI-assisted malware development signals the beginning of a new threat evolution cycle. Defenders must adopt AI-powered analysis tools to maintain detection parity.
