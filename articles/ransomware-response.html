Ransomware in the Enterprise:
A Consultant's Field Account

Inside a real-world double-extortion ransomware attack on a Tier-1 insurance group — the containment decisions made under fire, the attribution methodology, and the hard lessons that every organization should internalize before the breach alarm sounds.

February 2023 9 min read Joe Smith-Essang Threat Response & Forensics

Client Confidentiality Notice

All client-identifying information has been anonymized. The technical methodology, attack indicators, and response playbook are drawn from direct engagement experience and are shared here purely for educational purposes.

Executive Summary

A Tier-1 insurance group — one of the largest in the region by asset base — was struck by the BlackCat/ALPHV ransomware syndicate in a double-extortion attack. The group's entire enterprise network, including connected online backup repositories, was encrypted. I was engaged as the incident response lead to contain the threat, attribute the attack, coordinate forensic preservation, and supervise the recovery process in compliance with the applicable sectoral regulatory framework.

This article distils that engagement into a replicable methodology and a set of hard-won recommendations that any security practitioner or executive can apply to strengthen their organization's ransomware posture before an incident occurs.

Attack Timeline

Reconstructing the adversary's kill chain from forensic artifacts revealed a methodical, multi-phase intrusion that preceded the detonation of the ransomware payload by several days.

T-7 days (estimated)

Initial Access

Adversary establishes foothold, likely via phishing or exploitation of an internet-facing service with valid credentials. Exact vector confirmed through forensic artefacts preserved from hypervisor snapshots.

T-5 to T-3 days

Discovery & Lateral Movement

BlackCat uses built-in Living-off-the-Land (LotL) tools — net commands, WMI, BloodHound-style AD enumeration — to map the network and identify high-value targets and backup infrastructure.

T-2 to T-1 days

Credential Harvesting & Backup Access

Domain Administrator credentials harvested. Online backup servers accessed and shadow copies deleted. Exfiltration of sensitive policyholder data begins to attacker-controlled infrastructure.

T-0 — Detonation

Mass Encryption Initiated

BlackCat/ALPHV payload deployed across all reachable hosts via GPO abuse and remote service execution. Ransom notes dropped in every directory. Operations come to a complete halt.

T+0 hrs

IR Engagement Initiated

Consultant engaged. Immediate triage begins to assess scope, isolate affected segments, and engage the national CERT for public agency coordination.

T+4 hrs

Containment Achieved

All affected network segments isolated. IOCs extracted and distributed to all perimeter controls. Hypervisor snapshots taken for forensic preservation. Recovery planning initiated from offline backups.

Six-Phase Response Methodology

The response was executed across six distinct phases, each building on the previous. Speed of containment and forensic fidelity were treated as equally important objectives.

01

Immediate Network Isolation

The first and most time-critical action is severing the adversary's active access. All affected network segments were immediately isolated at the switch level to prevent further lateral movement. Internet egress from infected segments was blocked at the firewall. Business-critical systems on unaffected segments remained operational under enhanced monitoring.

ACTION: Block inter-VLAN routing from infected segments → core switch ACLs
ACTION: Disable affected AD accounts & revoke Kerberos tickets (klist purge)
ACTION: Kill VPN sessions from all remote endpoints pending assessment
02

Forensic Snapshot Preservation

Critical lesson: before any recovery operation begins, forensic-grade snapshots of all affected VMs must be taken at the hypervisor level. This is non-negotiable. Recovery wipes the forensic artefacts needed to reconstruct the attack timeline, attribute the actor, and support regulatory investigations.

ACTION: Create VM snapshots on ESXi/Hyper-V — do NOT power off without snapshot
ACTION: Export raw disk images (.vmdk / .vhd) to isolated forensic storage
ACTION: Document chain of custody for all forensic artefacts
03

Threat Actor Attribution

Attribution was achieved by analysing the dropped ransom note, correlating file extension patterns, and cross-referencing IOCs against threat intelligence repositories. BlackCat's Rust-based payload and its characteristic “RECOVER-<extension>-FILES.txt” naming convention confirmed the actor. IOC packages were then shared with the national CERT and sectoral regulatory body.

TOOL: OSForensics — hash extraction & malware artefact recovery
TOOL: Volatility — memory forensics to recover in-memory payload artifacts
TOOL: RegRipper — registry timeline analysis for persistence mechanisms
04

Multi-Layer IOC Blocking

Extracted indicators of compromise (file hashes, IP addresses, domain names, ransom note filenames) were pushed to every available control layer to prevent re-infection and cut any remaining C2 channels.

LAYER 1 (Endpoint): Hash blacklisting via EDR policy push
LAYER 2 (Email Gateway): Domain/sender reputation rules updated
LAYER 3 (DNS): Sinkholing C2 domains at internal resolver
LAYER 4 (Firewall/Proxy): IP & URL blocklist enrichment
LAYER 5 (SIEM): Detection rules for re-appearance of IOC pattern
05

Supervised Credential Reset & Access Rebuild

Because domain credentials were confirmed compromised, a supervised enterprise-wide credential reset was mandatory before any recovery could begin. All privileged accounts were reset first, followed by standard user accounts. MFA was enforced on all remote access paths before any system was reconnected to the network.

PRIORITY ORDER: Domain Admin → Service Accounts → Standard Users
ACTION: Force password reset at next logon via AD policy
ACTION: Enroll all admin accounts into hardware MFA (FIDO2 tokens)
ACTION: Audit service account SPNs for Kerberoastable accounts
06

Offline Backup Restoration & Post-Incident Reporting

Recovery was achieved exclusively from offline (air-gapped) backups — the online backup infrastructure had been encrypted along with the rest of the estate. Systems were rebuilt in order of business criticality. Post-incident, a formal maturity assessment was conducted to identify the gaps that allowed the attack to succeed, and a regulatory notification package was prepared per applicable sectoral requirements.

OUTCOME: Full recovery without ransom payment
TOOL: CrowdStrike / Cortex XDR EDR deployed on all recovered endpoints before reconnection
DELIVERABLE: Incident timeline report + regulatory notification package

Key Lessons from the Field

1. Online Backups Are Not Backups

The most dangerous misconception in enterprise resilience is equating online backups with disaster recovery capability. BlackCat specifically targeted and encrypted the backup infrastructure before detonating the main payload. Any backup solution that is reachable from the corporate network — including cloud sync, NAS shares, and backup agents on domain-joined machines — is within the ransomware's blast radius.

Critical Finding

Online backups were encrypted alongside the production environment, eliminating that recovery path entirely. Offline, air-gapped backups were the only mechanism that enabled recovery without paying the ransom.

2. Forensic Preservation Must Precede Recovery

There is an inherent tension between the IR team's desire to restore services quickly and the need to preserve forensic evidence. The organization's instinct was to immediately begin wiping and rebuilding infected machines. Intercepting that instinct and forcing the creation of hypervisor snapshots first proved essential — it was from those snapshots that the attack timeline was reconstructed and the regulatory package was built.

3. Active Directory Is the Attacker's Prize

BlackCat's pivot to AD-wide encryption was enabled by harvested Domain Administrator credentials. The speed with which the actor moved from initial access to full AD compromise indicated either an existing weak credential policy, a lack of Privileged Access Management (PAM) controls, or both. Without AD, nothing else matters — it is the foundational target.

4. The National CERT Is a Force Multiplier

Early notification of the national CERT proved valuable. The CERT provided supplementary threat intelligence, confirmed the attribution, and facilitated coordination with the sectoral regulatory body. Organizations often delay this notification out of reputational concern — this is a mistake. Early coordination reduces the overall response time significantly.

Recommendations for Your Organization

These are not theoretical controls. They are the specific gaps that this engagement exposed, formulated as actionable recommendations for any organization seeking to reduce its ransomware risk.

Implement Offline & Immutable Backups

Follow the 3-2-1-1-0 rule: 3 copies, 2 media types, 1 off-site, 1 offline (air-gapped), 0 errors (tested). Test restoration quarterly — untested backups are not backups.

Segment Your Network

Micro-segment OT, backup infrastructure, and high-value asset zones from the general corporate network. Lateral movement should require explicit firewall policy, not default trust.

Enforce MFA on All Remote Access

VPN, RDP, and privileged admin portals must require MFA. Hardware FIDO2 tokens for privileged accounts eliminate credential-only attacks on administrative paths.

Deploy Privileged Access Management (PAM)

Use just-in-time (JIT) privilege elevation rather than standing Domain Admin accounts. PAM vaults, session recording, and privileged account discovery are essential controls.

EDR on Every Endpoint — No Exceptions

Behavioural EDR (CrowdStrike, Cortex XDR, SentinelOne) catches ransomware TTPs that signature-based AV misses. Coverage must be 100% — unmanaged endpoints are the attack surface.

Test Your Ransomware IR Playbook

A playbook on paper is not a playbook. Conduct tabletop exercises and purple team simulations specifically for ransomware scenarios to validate your response capability before the real event.

Subscribe to Threat Intelligence Feeds

IOC feeds (MISP, commercial TI platforms) give your SOC early warning of campaigns targeting your sector. BlackCat TTPs were well-documented — intelligence ahead of the attack changes the outcome.

Monitor for Backup Deletion Events

Alert on vssadmin delete shadows, wbadmin delete catalog, and equivalent storage API calls. Backup deletion is a pre-encryption signal that provides a response window.

Key Takeaways

  • Containment speed is the only variable you control after the payload detonates — network isolation within the first hour limits the blast radius dramatically.
  • Forensic snapshots before recovery is a non-negotiable principle: without them, the regulatory and legal process cannot be completed, and attribution is lost.
  • Offline backups are the ultimate insurance policy — they are the single control that enabled a full recovery without ransom payment in this case.
  • AD credential hygiene and PAM are the highest-return pre-incident investments for ransomware defence; every major RaaS operator targets AD first.
  • CERT and regulator relationships built before an incident dramatically reduce response time — make those connections now, not during a crisis.

Outcome

The organization achieved full recovery from offline backups with zero ransom payment. Post-incident, a comprehensive security maturity assessment was delivered, a formal EDR deployment programme was initiated, and backup architecture was overhauled to an air-gapped, immutable design. A regulatory notification was filed within the required timeframe.
